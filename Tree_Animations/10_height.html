Read the formula out loud

height(r) = 1 + max( height(r→left), height(r→right) )
“My height is one more than my taller child’s height.” The max is there because a downward path cannot use both children at once. The +1 is the edge from me to that child.
Why the recursion must go bottom-up. A node cannot know its height without knowing its children's heights, so nothing can be answered on the way down. Watch the badges appear: leaves get their number first, the root last. That order is postorder.
The two conventions. With h(NULL)=0 you are counting nodes on the longest path; with h(NULL)=-1 you are counting edges. The recursive line is identical — only the base case moves. Switch the buttons above and watch every badge shift by exactly one. LeetCode 104 wants nodes.

Minimum depth is not 1 + min(L, R). For a node with one child, that formula returns 1 and pretends the missing side is a leaf. A leaf needs both children NULL. This is the single most failed easy tree problem.

Complexity

      
TimeO(N)
SpaceO(H)
Each node runs its body once and does constant work, so O(N). The space is the call stack, O(H). Compare with the naive way of answering “how tall is each subtree?” by calling height() again at every node — that recomputes the same subtrees over and over and costs O(N²).
